About the brand

Every path leads somewhere. We help you find it.

True North is a luxury fine art photography brand. The work is landscape-driven, cinematic, and place-based; built for people who want more than decoration on their walls.

Every image is earned on foot, often miles from the nearest road, in glowing light that only lasts about thirty seconds. The photographs are limited edition, every edition is intentional, and the experience of owning one should feel like you were there too.

Right now, the site works. But it's lost the plot (pun intended) by focusing on scents and apparel. Your job is to close that gap... and then some.

The audience

Our customers are "seekers."

"They look for unique, one-of-a-kind items, and don't want mass-produced decor. They want a piece from a trail they've been on before, that becomes a story they can hang on their wall."

True North customers are outdoor enthusiasts, collectors, and hikers who value the story behind the art. They are driven by experience and authenticity. They buy slowly and deliberately. When they invest in a piece, it means they have a personal connection to it.

They might encounter the brand by meeting the artist on a trail in a national park, on Instagram during a late-night scroll, or through a friend who already owns a print. However they arrive, the brand needs to feel immediately credible, and worth their time.

The Collector
Values scarcity, provenance, and craft. Researches before buying. Wants to feel they discovered something no one else has.
The Hiker
Discovered the brand while on trail. Made a personal connection to the artist. Responds to stories and places they've been.
The Gifter
Buying for someone who just bought a home, moved, or remodeled. Needs confidence the piece will be special and arrive safely.
The Decorator
Needs the ability to project the artwork on a client's wall in real time to get the client to understand the vision and commit.
About the artist

The miles behind the work.

More than eleven years ago, True North founder Elena Marsh picked up a camera for the first time and fell in love. Every week, she would hike the ridgelines and river canyons near her home in the high desert, photographing light moving across rock, and the long shadows that only show up an hour before sunset.

Photography became her discipline. She studied the masters, practiced obsessively, and returned to the same locations over and over to test new compositions, exposure times, and lighting conditions until she got it just right.

She met her creative partner, Becca Lakin, on a group backpacking trip in 2017, and from the beginning they bonded over a shared instinct to get in the truck and drive until they found somewhere new, never taking the same road twice. That instinct has carried the studio to 2 countries, 22 states, 31 national parks, and more than 210,000 miles of road.

Follow the journey on Instagram @truenorth.prints. And if you have questions about national parks, hike recommendations, or where to go for a new adventure, send a message. By this point, the team is fairly qualified to answer.

Scope of work

Your challenge.

This semester picks up a project already in motion: True North has a full desktop redesign in Figma, and it's ready for a team to prototype, test, and build. Choose one of the four roles below to focus on for the semester. Whichever you pick, remember this: it's a gallery that happens to sell things, not a store that happens to look nice. True North prides itself in not using AI to generate any work for its brand — we want to retain that hand-crafted quality, because it's something our customers value, and that same care should show up in your process too.

01Research & Testing

This team makes sure the redesign is judged by real users, not just by us. You'll get the new Figma file clickable, then put it in front of people and report back what actually happened.

  • Competitive analysis. Work through the provided competitive analysis document and get familiar with each competitor — their strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats (SWOT).
  • Prototype the redesign. Wire up the provided desktop Figma file so every page is clickable and ready to test.
  • Test the redesign. Usability test the new prototype with at least 10 users to gather qualitative and quantitative feedback.
  • Report back. Synthesize your findings into clear, prioritized recommendations for the rest of the team.
Research questions to answer during step 4

We're especially interested in how users read the size selector on the fine art product page (Studio, Gallery, Statement, Collector). Build your test around these questions:

  1. Do users understand that Studio, Gallery, Statement, and Collector represent different physical sizes, without being told?
  2. When users want the actual dimensions, do they know where to look, and do they find them?
  3. Does the name-only selector work on its own, or do users need dimensions built into the size buttons?
  4. Where in the purchase flow do users feel uncertain about how to actually buy a print?
  5. Where do users feel they need more information, and what kind of information would they want?
  6. What's their overall reaction to the site — what works, what doesn't, what surprised them?
Resources Usability Test Study Summary Spreadsheet →
Your job isn't to confirm the redesign works. It's to find out, honestly, where it doesn't — before a customer does.
02Mobile

The desktop redesign already exists in Figma. Your job is to prove it holds up everywhere else, and to build the components that make that possible.

  • Competitive analysis. Work through the provided competitive analysis document and get familiar with each competitor's SWOT, same as the research team.
  • Map the site. Build a sitemap and a user-flow diagram of the redesigned website based on the desktop pages already in Figma, so you understand how every page connects before you touch a component.
  • Build a responsive component library. Pull every individual component out of the provided desktop layouts and rebuild it so it scales cleanly from mobile up to desktop, and back down again.
  • Build the mobile wireframe. Use your responsive component library to construct the mobile wireframe.
  • Prototype the mobile wireframe. Make it clickable. (The research team is handling the desktop prototype, so this one's yours.)
Resources Responsive scaling walkthrough → Component library playlist →
A responsive component isn't a shrunk-down desktop component. It's the same idea, redrawn for a different frame. The mobile site MUST have all the same elements and pieces as the desktop.
03Development

This team builds the front end that gets this redesign as close to production-ready as possible. You're building from what the research and mobile teams validate and hand off, not designing from scratch. You won't have direct access to a live Shopify store for this project — but every decision should be made knowing that Shopify is where this site ultimately goes.

  • Set up the workflow. Stand up a front-end build (HTML/CSS/JS) structured the way a Shopify theme would be — reusable sections and templates — and use GitHub for version control. Agree on a branching and review process with your team before writing code.
  • Build to the Figma file. Translate the provided high-fidelity desktop designs into working front-end templates and components — matching layout, type, color, and spacing exactly.
  • Build in components. Build reusable sections and blocks for anything that repeats (product cards, size/frame selectors, print detail layouts) rather than one-off page templates.
  • Bring the AR viewer to life. The redesign includes an AR room viewer. Research and recommend a Shopify-compatible solution (native .usdz/.glb, Zakeke, Threekit, or other), and prioritize iPhone, since that's where most customers will be.
  • Take the mobile hand-off. Once the mobile team's component library and wireframe are ready, extend the build to match those breakpoints.
  • QA against the design. Cross-check your build against the Figma file and the research team's findings, and fix discrepancies before calling anything done.
Every pixel that drifts from the Figma file is a small argument against the brand. Build like someone is going to hold your work up next to the design and compare.
04Social & Content

True North has no existing social media presence — there's no account to inherit, no cadence to keep, no voice already in motion. This team starts from zero and builds the strategy that gets the brand discovered in the first place, with Reels as the primary vehicle.

  • Competitive analysis. Work through the same competitive set as the rest of the project — but read them for how they show up in Reels, Stories, and captions, not just on their websites.
  • Define the starting point. Since there's nothing live to audit, decide what True North's first 90 days on social should look like: platform priorities, content pillars, and posting cadence, built from the brand strategy and audience personas, not from an existing account.
  • Develop reel concepts. Come up with several distinct storyboard approaches for Instagram Reels, each built to launch and grow the account from scratch and pull people toward the new site, not just rack up views.
  • Storyboard each concept. Frame by frame: what's on screen, what's said (if anything), how long each beat runs, and what real footage or stills it draws from.
  • Write the hooks and captions. Draft the opening line, caption, and call-to-action for each reel — in True North's voice, not a generic brand voice.
  • Present a point of view on cadence. Beyond the individual reels, give the client a recommendation on launch sequencing, recurring themes, and how social ties back to the print catalog and the artist's story.
A reel with no story behind it is just footage. Every concept has to feel like the thirty seconds of light we hiked for — real places, real shots, no AI-generated or stock imagery in the final brand voice, same as every other deliverable on this project.

Photography direction

True North photography is earned in the miles it takes to get it. The images are made in conditions most people don't even explore. We set out on a 10-mile trail at golden hour or before dawn breaks. We venture down rivers, across deserts, up mountains, and through canyons. The art direction of every asset should carry the same sense of adventure.

On-brand ✓
  • Natural light, no flash
  • Landscape-first framing
  • Cinematic mood
  • Place-specific, earned shots
Off-brand ✕
  • AI-generated imagery
  • Over-processed, HDR look
  • Stock photo aesthetics
  • Generic travel photography

Metrics for success

Finding True North: How we'll know we've arrived?

There are six performance indicators for this project. Each team will be evaluated on all six. The winning concepts will be strong across all of them.

01

On-time delivery

All deliverables completed and presented by each milestone deadline. 100% of the work, on time, every time.

The client who waits for your deck is already thinking about your replacement.

02

Conceptual cohesion

Every deliverable — from the usability research, to the mobile components, to the working build — feels like it came from the same mind. No fragmented projects.

Great research can't save a bad build. They rise together or they sink together.

03

Feature integrity

The experience is fully thought through. Edge cases addressed. Flows complete. Nothing left half-built.

The moments a user doesn't expect to hit are exactly the ones that decide whether they trust you.

04

Quality of execution

Pixel-perfect, well-organized, and production-ready. Files that could be handed to a Shopify developer without a follow-up email.

A developer opening your Figma file should feel relief, not dread.

05

Strategic depth

Decisions are traceable back to research and brand strategy — not just aesthetic preference. The team can defend every call.

"It looked good" is not a strategy. "Our collector persona needs trust signals above the fold" is.

06

Creative risk

The concept does something the competition hasn't done, or does something familiar in a way that is distinctly True North.

Safe work wins nothing. The brief says "design like it's going live." Take that seriously.

Pro tips from the client

Straight from the trail.

  • Every image in the True North catalog was earned on foot — often miles from the nearest road, in light that lasts about thirty seconds. That is the story. Every design decision you make should reinforce that the work is genuine, hard-won, and irreplaceable. If the design starts to feel like stock photography or generic e-commerce, it has lost the thread.

  • If a buyer has to hunt for the AR option, or if it feels like a bonus feature tacked on at the end of the purchase flow, it has not been designed well enough. The best version of this feature makes the customer feel like they are standing in their own living room with a print in their hands — before they have spent a dollar. Design the experience around that feeling, not around the technology.

  • The photographs are real because the locations are real and the miles were real. AI-generated imagery undermines that entirely. The sizzle reel deliverable must use real footage — no AI generation, no synthetic landscapes, no composited scenes that did not happen. This applies to every visual deliverable in the project. When in doubt, ask.
